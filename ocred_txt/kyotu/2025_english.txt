Animal Sleep Patterns
If you ever spend all day at home, you might notice that your pet cat
sleeps
a
lot during the day and
is
quite

Humans, on the other hand, are awake during most of the daylight

Sleep is essential for animals’ physical and mental health, and for their
bodies to function efficiently.
For animals with brains and central nervous

sleep

Sleep also gives the brain neurons

Most creatures experience

Different
sleep

over
a
24hour

in
various types

Three common patterns

Monophasic sleep, experienced by humans and many
larger animals, happens when one is awake and active for many hours, and
then one sleeps for an extended period.
Some birds, insects, and mammals

times, with one sleep being long and the other like a nap.
Dogs and cats are

six

There are variations

Smaller animals such as squirrels or mice tend to use
up their energy by moving quickly and frequently.
This results in the need to
sleep more

time.
Lions
are carnivorous

Safety is another variable in sleep patterns.
Animals that can create safe

Apes sleep on platforms, high above the jungle floor, which

Some smaller animals such as rabbits create

In contrast, elephants

The animal sleep patterns discussed so far tend to be typical.
A pattern
that may be less easily noticed in our daily lives is called unihemispheric sleep.
In this type of sleep, some animals traveling in a group keep one eye open.
While one side of the animal’s brain sleeps, the other side stays awake and
alert to its surroundings.
In this way, the animal can experience the reviving
effects of sleep while also watching out for threats.
This unihemispheric sleep

Birds flying at the outer edge of the group use this type of sleep to help

Besides the types of sleep explained above, there are patterns that can be
considered to be sleeplike activities.
Hibernation, which can be observed in

During this time, the animal’s body temperature drops, its heart rate
